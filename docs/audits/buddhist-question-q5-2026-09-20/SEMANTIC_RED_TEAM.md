## Claim-level attacks

- **“The Bodhi tree mattered only because the Buddha spent time under it”:** rejected. Q5 centers the awakening association and treats later use as an additional narrative connection.
- **“Ja 479 ranks the tree above every other object”:** rejected. The page says the text contains no scored comparison with a hut, bed, chair, or seat.
- **“The Buddha ordered Bodhi-tree worship everywhere”:** rejected. The claim remains bounded to the later Theravāda Jetavana narrative.
- **“Older tree worship proves Ānanda’s motive”:** rejected. Met material is labelled cultural and art-historical context only.
- **“A sacred quality is scientifically transmitted through propagation”:** rejected. Continuity is described through lineage, dedication, symbolism, and recollection.
- **“The tree was prettier, theft-proof, fireproof, or waterproof”:** rejected. These unsupported and absolute claims do not appear.
- **“The planting trained all future Buddhists to worship Bodhi trees”:** rejected. No verified source used here supplies that motive.

## Source-layer attacks

- **Jātaka prose presented as an early discourse:** rejected. Ja 479 is visibly classified as “Theravāda narrative/commentary.”
- **UNESCO presented as Buddhist doctrine:** rejected. It is classified as “Cultural heritage / history” and used narrowly for the Bodh Gaya site and awakening association.
- **Museum context presented as narrative motive:** rejected. The Met source supports wider tree-shrine history but not Ānanda’s intent.
- **Editorial synthesis hidden as scripture:** rejected. Propagation, visibility, and communal-focus observations are explicitly labelled as Echo Buddha interpretation.
