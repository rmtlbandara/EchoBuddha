## Claim-level attacks

- **“Appaṭimo simply means ‘no statue’”:** rejected. The early term is read inside AN 1.174’s cluster of incomparable and peerless epithets.
- **“AN 1.174 prohibits images”:** rejected. The discourse contains no such rule and is not described as a statue discussion.
- **“The commentary says people must not make images”:** rejected. It acknowledges human-made images while denying perfect bodily likeness.
- **“If an image is inexact, it cannot represent”:** rejected. The page explicitly distinguishes representation from physical duplication.
- **“One regional style preserves the Buddha’s exact portrait”:** rejected. Art history is used to show variation and convention, not photographic identity.
- **“The material is literally the Buddha”:** rejected. The page consistently distinguishes object, representation, and represented subject.
- **“Any damage is desecration”:** rejected. Intent and context distinguish deliberate contempt from accident, conservation, restoration, and study.

## Source-layer attacks

- **Commentary presented as early discourse:** rejected. `Theravāda commentary` is a separate visible source type.
- **Museum presented as doctrinal authority:** rejected. Met sources support only regional variation and iconographic convention.
- **Editorial analogy hidden as scripture:** rejected. Representation/duplication and respectful-context explanations are Echo Buddha synthesis.
- **Height story used as early or physical evidence:** rejected. It is omitted from the public argument and documented only as later, unnecessary research context.
