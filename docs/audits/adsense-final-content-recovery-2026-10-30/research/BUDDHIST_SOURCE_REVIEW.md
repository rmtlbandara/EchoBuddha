## Five agent editorial passes

1. **Factual and source validation:** Compared the revised search narrative against MN 26 and the Sangha definition against AN 11.12 plus the source overview. SN 56.11 is no longer asked to support a complete biography. The four-sights account is explicitly a traditional retelling. No translated passage is reproduced as Echo Buddha prose.
2. **Reader usefulness and information gain:** The biography now helps a beginner assess life-story claims. The dictionary explains which sense of Sangha is intended and how this affects joining a center versus understanding refuge. These additions address identified ambiguities rather than a word target.
3. **Natural language, originality, and repetition:** Read the two revised objects in their surrounding context. Source summaries and practical questions are original editorial explanation. Neither page becomes a full duplicate of the handbook or article; links provide the longer treatment.
4. **Search intent, links, and metadata:** Existing route/title/topic purpose preserved. The new handbook link resolves to the source-controlled `sangha-as-refuge` object. Modification dates apply only to these changed learning records; the original date remains unknown. Final built metadata and sitemap checks belong to release QA.
5. **Safety, accuracy, and publication readiness:** The community guidance discourages coercion and unsupported attainment claims. The biography does not require readers to treat textual narratives as independently verified history. No credentials, original quotations, spiritual guarantees, or clinical claims were added. `npm run typecheck` with Node 22.23.4 completed successfully.

## Other focused observations

[NCCIH's meditation and mindfulness safety overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety), retrieved October 30, says potential harms remain incompletely studied and describes reported negative experiences. Its discussion also cautions about preliminary or overly optimistic benefit interpretations. This supports proportionate uncertainty and adverse-experience boundaries. It does not establish that a brief Echo Buddha exercise treats anxiety, insomnia, trauma, or another condition, and it does not make every meditation universally safe. Existing adaptation/stop options and professional-support boundaries should remain.

Existing source/application boundaries for kamma (AN 6.63), sati (SN 48.10/DN 22), compassion (AN 4.125 versus Sn 1.8), and the four source-study notes are comparatively careful and should be preserved. The handbook's Sangha chapter already distinguishes noble, monastic, and broader usage; the shorter dictionary correction restores consistency with it. These observations are not a comprehensive scholarly certification of those collections.

The historical Buddha's exact dates and the historical development of every biographical motif were not independently researched here and were not added. Disputed doctrine, translation differences, commentary chronology, and media rights must not be treated as settled by this narrow review. Remaining full-site reader-value, duplication, quote/reflection, and source-link checks are recorded by the other audit workstreams.
