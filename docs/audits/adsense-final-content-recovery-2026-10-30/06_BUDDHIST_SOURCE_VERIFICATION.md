# Buddhist source verification and editorial boundaries

October 30, 2026 (Asia/Colombo). This report records completed repository research and editorial work. Build, hosted CI, deployment, live identity, and final production verification are separate release evidence.

## Scope and method

The source-review agent read the complete generated primary bodies of all **61 learning detail pages and nine meditation detail pages**, including their source notes, exercises, key boxes, and questions. It also read all **43 longer authored quote stories**, every key and all three prose fields in the **110 individualized short-quote applications**, and the complete bodies of the eight articles initially flagged for missing external references. Those populations overlap with the site's inventory; they must not be added as if each represented a new URL.

Page-level findings and internal eight-dimension assessments are in [the learning/meditation review](research/LEARNING_MEDITATION_BODY_REVIEW.json), [43-story review](research/AUTHORED_QUOTE_REVIEW.csv), and [110-application review](research/QUOTE_APPLICATION_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.csv). Full primary-prose reading snapshots remain under ignored `.artifacts/adsense-final-content-recovery-2026-10-30/editorial-reading/`; the learning register records their SHA-256 digests. Changed source prose was read again after the generated-body baseline.

This is **Codex agent review**, including a different agent from the quote-map author. It is not independent human, monastic, academic, translation-specialist, or clinical certification. All page bodies received the stated reading, but not every historical or doctrinal statement was independently retranslated or checked against every cited edition. Selected high-risk passages were retrieved directly and compared; references elsewhere were reviewed for fit, labeling, and appropriate scope.

Sources are used for the claim they actually support. Early discourses, canonical hagiography, later commentary, traditional manuals, modern scholarship, museum descriptions, regional practice, and original editorial examples remain distinct. A citation count never substitutes for that distinction.

## Four new claim-specific article anchors

[The eight-article review](research/ARTICLE_SOURCE_QUEUE_REVIEW.md) separates missing citations from original practical guidance. Four articles had explicit textual/doctrinal claims needing a direct anchor:

| Article | Added direct reference and scope |
| --- | --- |
| `dhammapada-reflection-trained-mind` | [Dhammapada 33–43](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/KN/Dhp/Ch03.html), especially training/guarding themes. Daily-life examples and evening review are original applications. |
| `how-to-let-go-of-attachment-in-buddhism` | [SN 12.2](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN12_2.html) for craving and clinging. The control/outcome examples are not the complete fourfold clinging analysis. |
| `walking-meditation-step-by-step` | [MN 10](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN10.html) for knowing posture and alertness in movement. Route length, labels, and turn drill are contemporary practical adaptation. |
| `three-ways-to-practice-patience` | [Dhammapada 184](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/KN/Dhp/Ch14.html) for endurance and non-harming. The three drills are not an invented canonical triad. |

The other four articles mainly supply modest original habit, home-space, listening, and morning-routine guidance. They were not given arbitrary citations to meet a quota. The implementation trims repeated expansions while preserving distinct methods, troubleshooting, and safety.

Further source/context corrections preserve the fifth-precept undertaking in its longer article; identify the mettā script's practice phrases as commonly used modern phrases and soften an unqualified psychological-effect assertion; explain noble-refuge versus broader community usage in the community article; and correct the temple article's copied source-context description. These changes do not claim that a modern script is a literal translation or that every traditional formulation is identical.

## Quotes, fiction, and wellbeing language

All 43 authored stories remain original reflective vignettes with explicit imagined-example disclosure. Dialogue within them is not historical testimony. Their source/origin record continues to identify original Echo Buddha editorial writing. The 110 short applications now address their exact quote through distinct interpretations, actions, and questions; they are not traditional quotations or externally verified clinical instructions. [The separate adversarial review](research/QUOTE_APPLICATION_ADVERSARIAL_REVIEW.md) found no material semantic mismatch, fabricated authority, or treatment guarantee in the completed map.

The shared quote context permits changing anchors, keeping eyes open, or stopping unfamiliar breath/body exercises. Anxiety, grief, intrusive memories, forgiveness, real danger, and medical-symptom examples retain specific limits. [NCCIH's safety overview](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/meditation-and-mindfulness-effectiveness-and-safety) supports acknowledging uncertain benefits and adverse experiences; it does not establish universal suitability or efficacy for these website exercises.

The work received separate agent passes for factual/source support; reader usefulness and information gain; natural language and repetition; intent, links, and metadata; and final safety/attribution/publication boundaries. No further material source or safety defect was identified in this agent's completed review after the stated corrections. That is a bounded editorial finding, not proof that no error exists. Exact textual chronology, disputed interpretations, Pāli/Sanskrit translation judgments, historical media entitlements, and worldwide originality remain outside a full scholarly or rights certification. Ancient themes do not automatically make a modern translation public domain. No broad copyright clearance, credentials, expert endorsement, clinical claim, Google approval, or future indexing result is asserted.
