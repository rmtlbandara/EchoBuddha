## Karma

Both pages currently define intention, distinguish karma from fate, explain repeated habits and mixed motives, use a workplace conflict, and discuss responsibility without shame. The clearest improvement is to retain AN 6.63 as the foundation on the Buddhism 101 page and give the article a repeatable method for examining a real choice.

MN 61 supplies the method of reflecting before, during and after action. A practical example could follow an accidental edit to a shared file and the subsequent choice to disclose, correct or conceal it. Before acting, consider likely harm. During the action, stop or change course if harm becomes apparent. Afterward, examine the actual effects, disclose harm where appropriate, and make a concrete change. MN 61 addresses Rāhula and distinguishes the response to harmful bodily or verbal action from reflection on a harmful mental action. The modern workplace repair steps are an application. [MN 61](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN61.html)

AN 6.63 establishes the role of intention in bodily, verbal and mental action and includes a wider account of kamma and its results. The everyday application can distinguish accident, intent, observed effects and repair without claiming to calculate a karmic result. SN 36.21 supports the narrower statement that some feelings arise through causes other than past action. It cannot diagnose why a particular person experiences illness or misfortune. [AN 6.63](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/AN/AN6_63.html) · [SN 36.21](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN36_21.html)

## Mindfulness practice source

The November 10 candidate’s handwashing cue and three-day experiment are reasonable editorial applications. DN 22 places alertness during ordinary actions, including moving about, eating, talking and keeping silent, in a monastic body-contemplation passage. The candidate identifies that context and gives attention precedence to the activity when safety requires it. Preserve the separation between the short exercise, a separate habit-building article and interpersonal listening. [DN 22](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/DN/DN22.html)

The habit article already offers a scheduled one-to-five-minute practice, a weekly adjustment and a way to resume after missed days. It repeats the handwashing cue and another carry-over exercise. A stronger division gives the meditation page the action-in-progress cue, the habit article a worked example of adjusting a formal session, and the listening article a conversation exercise. The listening article already teaches checking one's understanding and respecting boundaries. This is a distinction between reader tasks and editorial examples.

## Compassion and Karuṇā

The English entry helps a reader decide what to offer someone in pain. Its colleague example asks permission before volunteering help, which gives it a distinct everyday task. The Karuṇā entry already identifies the early-discourse setting, but it repeats the neighboring entry's advice about pity, listening and boundaries. An improvement would guide readers through AN 4.125's cultivation of compassion in all directions and compare the four attitudes in one situation. Keep the directional language and the discourse's rebirth context visible. Treat the modern example as an illustration rather than a translated definition.
