# Handbook Source Ledger

Date checked: 2026-09-26

The supplied DOCX is a mandatory reference and coverage source, but its public provenance was not independently identified. The public handbook therefore uses it to discover topics and traditional claims, then relies on the following sources to classify and verify what is published.

| Source | Type | Claims supported | Limits applied |
|---|---|---|---|
| [DN 16 — Mahāparinibbāna Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/dn16/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Standard recollections of Buddha, Dhamma, Saṅgha; four pairs/eight individuals; relic, stupa, pilgrimage, and memorial context | Does not prove later image, cetiya-category, or commentarial claims |
| [MN 27 — Cūḷahatthipadopama Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/mn27/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Confidence in the Buddha followed by gradual training and direct knowledge | Used for the teacher-to-practice movement, not later biography |
| [MN 95 — Caṅkī Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/mn95/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Careful movement from confidence and investigation toward realization | Background verification; not quoted at length |
| [DN 21 — Sakka’s Questions](https://suttacentral.net/dn21/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Deva approaches Buddha as questioner; deva is not framed as creator or liberator | Does not describe every later deity custom |
| [DN 31 — Sigālovāda Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/dn31/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Reciprocal responsibilities between householders and spiritual teachers | Not treated as a modern safeguarding policy by itself |
| [AN 8.88 — Appasāda Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/an8.88/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Eight grounds for lay disapproval and a bounded withdrawal of social support | Does not authorize harassment, rumor, threats, or concealment from civil authorities |
| [Dhammapada 188–192](https://suttacentral.net/dhp179-196/en/sujato) | Theravāda canonical collection | Refuge in the Triple Gem joined to understanding the Four Noble Truths | Used without reproducing a modern translation |
| [AN 10.92 — Vera Sutta](https://suttacentral.net/an10.92/en/thanissaro) | Early Buddhist discourse | Verified confidence in the Triple Gem, four pairs/eight individuals, ethics, and stream-entry factors | Does not collapse Noble Saṅgha into all ordained people |
| [SN 48.12 — In Brief](https://suttacentral.net/sn48.12/en/sujato) | Early Buddhist discourse | Four stages of realization in relation to the spiritual faculties | Used as a doctrinal map, not to diagnose attainments |
| [The Threefold Refuge — BPS publication](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/nyanaponika/wheel076.html) | Traditional Theravāda manual | Mundane/supramundane refuge; four forms; pollution and breach | Stable Access to Insight edition of the BPS work; labelled as later explanation, not one early sutta |
| [Going for Refuge & Taking the Precepts — BPS publication](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/bodhi/wheel282.html) | Traditional Theravāda manual | Meaning, function, and practice of refuge and lay ethical commitment | Stable Access to Insight edition of the BPS work; does not establish uniform ritual across all schools |
| [Refuge: An Introduction](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/thanissaro/refuge.html) | Traditional Theravāda manual | Non-creator framing and refuge as a path of training | Used as interpretation rather than neutral global survey |
| [Buddhist Ceremonies and Rituals of Sri Lanka — BPS publication](https://www.accesstoinsight.org/lib/authors/kariyawasam/wheel402.html) | Sri Lankan cultural practice | Refuge recitation, cetiya categories, ritual context, devales, offerings | Stable Access to Insight edition of the BPS work; does not support claims about every Sri Lankan Buddhist |
| [A Treatise on the Pāramīs — BPS](https://bps.lk/book/a-treatise-on-the-paramis) | Theravāda commentary/tradition | Bodhisatta perfections and the later four-incalculables-plus-100,000-eons chronology | Official publication page; religious chronology is labelled as tradition, not modern historical dating |
| [SuttaCentral Dīgha Nikāya edition introduction](https://suttacentral.net/edition/dn/en/sujato/general_introduction) | Academic/editorial reference | Main Pāli collections and distinction between early discourses and later Abhidhamma treatises | Used for broad textual history, not sectarian authority |
| [Essence of Tipiṭaka — Vipassana Research Institute](https://www.tipitaka.org/eot.html) | Traditional Theravāda reference | Three baskets and seven-book Theravāda Abhidhamma organization | Exact numerical totals remain edition-dependent |
| [The Buddhist Viṣṇu — Columbia University Press](https://cup.columbia.edu/book/the-buddhist-visnu/9780231133234/) | Academic monograph | Historical assimilation and transformation of Viṣṇu in Sinhala Buddhist culture | Cultural history, not canonical authority or a claim about all Sri Lankan Buddhists |
| [The Kataragama Pilgrimage — Journal of Asian Studies](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C1287D1F0655BDA41865047647CFDE41/S0021911800141622a.pdf/the-kataragama-pilgrimage-hindu-buddhist-interaction-and-its-significance-in-sri-lankas-polyethnic-social-system.pdf) | Academic reference | Buddhist–Hindu interaction and the pilgrimage’s place in Sri Lanka’s plural social system | Used for cultural context, not doctrinal authority |
| [Saddhammopāyana — Journal of the Pali Text Society](https://palitextsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/JPTS_XII_4.pdf) | Academic edition/study of a later Pāli work | Twenty-one-part enumeration of improper ways of obtaining requisites and its parallels | Establishes a later lineage; it is not presented as one list from an early sutta |

## Claims deliberately not verified as public facts

- Exact public provenance and authorship of the supplied DOCX.
- The exact verse source for `Jīvitañca adhammena...`.
- The 91,800,537,000-precept enumeration.
- Universal, edition-independent totals for suttas, recitation units, and 84,000 Dhamma items.
- The Dīghāvuka flag/peta narrative and the oil, carpet, food, cesspit, and descendant-punishment stories.
- The exact source and translation of the four “fire / moral bhikkhu” stanzas in paragraphs 107–114.

These items remain documented in the source coverage audit. They are either omitted from public factual prose or described only as later/unverified tradition.
